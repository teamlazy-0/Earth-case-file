# Earth Case Files — Space Apps submission description (250 words)

Earth Case Files turns climate data into detective work. Click anywhere on Earth and the app opens a case file for that exact place, pulling 35 years of real NASA data to write a verdict and recommended actions.

The problem is that climate trends are hard to explore. Global averages hide local stories, and raw datasets overwhelm non-specialists. Our solution makes every user a trend detective. A dark mission-control map invites a click. The side panel then investigates four variables from NASA POWER: temperature, rainfall, sunlight, and humidity. Each tab shows the observed line with a dashed least-squares trend, three evidence cards for trend per decade, anomaly versus 1990 to 2000, and the latest full year, plus a plain-language story with a verdict and what to do next. Compare mode overlays two places in amber and blue. A satellite selector adds NASA GIBS MODIS Terra true-color and NDVI imagery with a four-year date slider, and the header shows a whole-Earth photo from NASA EPIC on DSCOVR.

Try it with the Bangladesh coast. Rainfall has risen by 2.77 millimetres per day per decade, up 203 percent versus the 1990s, while temperature stays flat at 26.17 degrees. The verdict is a wetter trend, and the action is drainage, flood maps, and storage above flood level.

Earth Case Files uses only live NASA POWER, GIBS, and EPIC data, with transparent least-squares math, full keyboard and screen-reader support, and no backend at all. One click, real evidence, a clear verdict for every place today.
